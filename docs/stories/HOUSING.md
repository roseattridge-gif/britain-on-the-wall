# How We Got Here — Housing

Housing is the third shared story and the first to make the distinction between public money, system output, market affordability and social outcome unavoidable.

The column grammar is unchanged. Each fiscal period shows government context, sparse sourced policy, UK Housing and communities expenditure, England net additional dwellings, the England median house-price-to-earnings ratio and England households in temporary accommodation. Actual measurement periods remain visible.

The July 2024 handover is chronology-safe. The incoming Labour government inherited falling net additions, improving but still high purchase affordability, and rising temporary accommodation. The 2024–25 supply and March 2025 homelessness observations substantially overlap conditions preceding the July and December 2024 planning reforms. Long lag metadata prevents a policy announcement being treated as completed homes.

Deterministic labels such as `SPEND UP · SUPPLY DOWN` and `SUPPLY DOWN · HOUSING NEED UP` are observations only. They always carry the explicit warning that co-movement is not causation. House prices and mortgage rates are not government performance scores, and no immigration-to-housing causal claim is made.

The generic story model gained only reusable missing capabilities: optional unavailable observations, policy owner/status, context type and expected lag. No fourth topic or new visual architecture was introduced.
