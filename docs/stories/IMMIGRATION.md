# How We Got Here — Immigration

Period: April 2021 to June 2025. Geography: United Kingdom.

## Government context

- Boris Johnson, Conservative: start of display to 6 September 2022.
- Liz Truss, Conservative: 6 September to 25 October 2022.
- Rishi Sunak, Conservative: 25 October 2022 to 5 July 2024.
- Keir Starmer, Labour: from 5 July 2024.

Government control establishes who held national authority. It is not attribution.

## Decisive policy events

- 1 July 2021: Graduate route opened (`documented-policy`).
- 15 February 2022: care workers became eligible for the Health and Care route (`documented-policy`).
- 1 January 2024: most taught-postgraduate student dependants restricted (`documented-policy`).
- 11 March 2024: new care-worker dependants restricted (`documented-policy`).
- 4 April 2024: general Skilled Worker threshold increased to £38,700 (`documented-policy`).
- 12 May 2025: incoming government published its immigration white paper (`documented-policy`; publication is not implementation).

## Measures and chronology

The primary measure is ONS long-term net migration, an official statistic in development. The revised series peaks at about 944,000 in the year ending March 2023. The latest suitable annual estimate immediately before the 5 July 2024 handover is 649,000 for the year ending June 2024: the trajectory was already falling. It falls further to a provisional 204,000 in the year ending June 2025.

The system/outcome layer deliberately avoids speculative housing, NHS or social-impact claims. No comparable Home Office spending or capacity series is shown because no sufficiently clear, like-for-like series was established for this compact pilot.

## Attribution boundary

ONS identifies fewer non-EU+ work- and study-related arrivals and rising emigration as drivers of the latest fall. The Migration Advisory Committee identifies work routes (especially health and social care), study routes and humanitarian routes as major contributors to the earlier post-2020 rise, and records the large fall in student dependants after the January 2024 restriction. These support contributor-level statements; they do not isolate a single policy effect or assign a party score.

The pre-handover 649,000 point is classified `followed-by`, not causal attribution. The post-handover 204,000 point overlaps policies, arrivals and departures from earlier periods. The May 2025 white paper was mostly prospective and cannot explain data that substantially predates implementation.

## Evidence summary

- **Observed:** sharp rise after 2021, revised peak in YE March 2023, falling before the July 2024 handover, then a further fall.
- **Policy context:** expanded graduate/care routes preceded the rise; student, care-dependant and salary restrictions took effect in early 2024; a further programme was published in May 2025.
- **Attribution:** authoritative sources support contributions from route composition, fewer work/study arrivals, fewer dependants and increased emigration.
- **Cannot say:** the evidence does not quantify each policy or government’s share of the change.
