The ONS affordability values are from the latest 2026 workbook rather than superseded bulletins: 9.06, 8.56, 8.45, 7.84 and 7.64. Lower is better, but 7.64 still means the median home cost more than seven-and-a-half times median annual full-time earnings.

## Interpretation boundary

Nominal UK expenditure rose in every column, from £15.273bn to £22.470bn, while its share of TME rose to 1.70% in 2023–24 and then eased to 1.65% by 2025–26. England net additions were broadly flat in 2022–23, then fell for two consecutive published years. Purchase affordability improved after the 2021 peak, while temporary accommodation rose every March. These are co-movements in differently scoped systems. They do not identify a spending multiplier, a party effect, or a causal chain from migration to prices or homelessness.
