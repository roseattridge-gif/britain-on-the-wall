# How We Got Here — Health

## Public question

How did health money, capacity, activity and elective-care performance move across the same five fiscal periods shown on the national Wall?

Health is the second configuration of the shared story system. The topic selector switches between Immigration and Health without changing the selected fiscal year. It does not create a new page, dashboard or Health-only interaction grammar.

## Five-second hierarchy

Each year column holds government context, at most one material policy event, UK-wide nominal Health & social care spending and share of total public money, then four England operational measures: workforce capacity, monthly completed RTT pathways, the waiting list and the percentage within 18 weeks.

The selected-year panel separates MONEY, CAPACITY, OUTPUT and OUTCOME. `ENGLAND OPERATIONAL MEASURE · UK SPEND` stays visible. Spending and capacity are context measures, never labelled as improved or declined.

## Handover and attribution

The 2024–25 column remains `Conservative → Labour` with `Handover: 5 July 2024`. The account distinguishes the state inherited at handover from movement observed later. Policy events are `documented-policy`; their position on the chronology is not a causal claim.

The deterministic contradiction detector may show `SPEND UP · PERFORMANCE DOWN` or `CAPACITY UP · PERFORMANCE DOWN` when those exact directions coexist. Every label carries the caveat that observed co-movement is not proof of cause.

## Limits

- UK public spending and England NHS operational statistics have different geographies and must not be presented as one denominator.
- RTT pathways are not unique patients.
- Completed pathways are a March monthly flow, not annual activity or quality.
- The March 2024 RTT series has a documented community-service scope change of about 43,000 pathways.
- Nominal spending is not inflation-adjusted and does not measure service quality or productivity.
